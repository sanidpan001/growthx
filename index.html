What is CAGR Calculator? Complete Guide 2026

CAGR stands for Compound Annual Growth Rate. It is the most accurate way to measure investment growth over time. Unlike simple average return, CAGR shows you the true yearly compounding rate at which your money grew. Our GrowthX CAGR Calculator is 100% free, no watermark, no login, unlimited usage worldwide. For example, you invested Rs 1 Lakh in a mutual fund and after 5 years it became Rs 2.5 Lakh. Simple return is 150% total, but CAGR is 20.11% per year. This means your money grew at 20.11% yearly compounded. This is the number every professional investor uses. Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway CAGR is 19.8% for 60 years, which made him billionaire.

CAGR Formula Explained with Example

CAGR Formula: CAGR = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1 / Number of Years) - 1. In percentage: CAGR% = [(EV/BV)^(1/n) -1] *100. Our calculator does this automatically. If you invested $10,000 in Apple stock in 2020 and now it is $28,000 in 2026 (6 years), CAGR = (28000/10000)^(1/6)-1 = 18.69% per year. This is better than bank FD which gives 7%.

Many people confuse CAGR with absolute return and simple average. Absolute return is just (Final-Initial)/Initial*100. For above example absolute is 150%. But CAGR gives you yearly rate. If two mutual funds both gave 150% in 5 years vs 3 years, their CAGR is different: 5-year CAGR 20.11%, 3-year CAGR 35.72%. So 3-year fund is better even though absolute same. That's why CAGR is rank #1 metric for comparison.

CAGR vs XIRR vs Absolute Return - Difference

CAGR is for one-time lump sum investment. XIRR is for SIP/multiple cashflows. Absolute return is total return without time. For example, you invested 1L, got 2L in 5 years: Absolute 100%, CAGR 14.87%. If you invested monthly SIP, you must use XIRR not CAGR. But for comparing two stocks or two businesses, CAGR is king. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q1. What is a good CAGR?
For stocks, 15%+ CAGR for 10 years is excellent. For mutual funds, 12-15% is very good. For business revenue, 20-30% CAGR is strong growth. Bank FD gives 7% CAGR.
Q2. How to calculate CAGR in Excel?
Formula in Excel: = (Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/Years) -1. Or use RATE function: =RATE(years,0,-beginning,ending). Our calculator does same but faster.
Q3. Is CAGR same as interest rate?
Yes, CAGR is effective annual interest rate with compounding. If bank says 7% compounded yearly, CAGR is 7%. But CAGR is used for volatile assets like stocks to smooth returns.
Q4. Can CAGR be negative?
Yes, if final value is less than initial, CAGR will be negative. Example: 100k to 80k in 2 years = -10.55% CAGR, meaning you lost 10.55% yearly.
